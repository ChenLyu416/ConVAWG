# Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) VAWG Crime Definitions

This document provides comprehensive definitions of crimes under the CPS Violence Against Women and Girls (VAWG) strategy, used for generating scenarios and personas in this research project.

## Introduction

The Crown Prosecution Service (CPS) groups various crimes under its Violence Against Women and Girls (VAWG) strategy. The crimes include:
- Domestic abuse
- Coercive control
- Stalking/harassment
- Sexual offences (including rape and child sexual abuse)
- Child abuse (non-sexual)
- So-called honour-based abuse (including forced marriage and harmful practices such as female genital mutilation)
- Prostitution & human trafficking
- Crimes relating to obscene publications

## Crime Definitions

### 1. Domestic Abuse and Controlling or Coercive Behaviour

#### Domestic Abuse
Domestic abuse is a pattern of abusive behaviour in an intimate or family relationship used by one person to gain or maintain power and control over another. Abuse can be psychological, physical, sexual, financial or emotional. Victims may not recognise non-physical abuse because the abuser may manipulate finances, isolate them from support or use humiliation and intimidation.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `physical`
- `psychological`
- `emotional`
- `economic`
- `financial`
- `sexual`

#### Controlling Behaviour
A range of acts designed to make a person subordinate and/or dependent by isolating them from sources of support, exploiting their resources and capacities for personal gain, depriving them of the means needed for independence, resistance and escape and regulating their everyday behaviour.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `coercive_control`

#### Coercive Behaviour (Section 76, Serious Crime Act 2015)
An offence where a suspect ("A") repeatedly or continuously engages in behaviour that is controlling or coercive towards another person ("B"), they are personally connected, the behaviour has a serious effect on B and A knows or ought to know that.

CCB often builds gradually; individual incidents may appear innocuous but together amount to abuse. Examples include:
- Isolating the victim from friends and family
- Depriving them of basic needs
- Monitoring their time or online activity
- Using smart devices or social media to coerce or upset them
- Controlling where they go, who they see and what they wear

**Related crime types in system:**
- `coercive_control`

### 2. Stalking and Harassment

#### Stalking
"Stalking" is understood as a pattern of **Fixated, Obsessive, Unwanted and Repeated (FOUR)** behaviour which is intrusive. 

Stalking/harassment often occurs online ("cyberstalking"), where perpetrators use social networking sites, email or other digital tools to locate personal information, communicate with the victim, monitor them and damage their reputation.

Examples of stalking include:
- Frequent unwanted contact (visiting the victim's home or workplace, sending numerous texts/emails)
- Following or watching the victim
- Sending unwanted gifts
- Damaging property
- Boasting about knowing the victim's location
- Issuing threats of physical or sexual harm

**Related crime types in system:**
- `stalking`
- `cyberstalking`
- `digital`

#### Harassment
Repeated behaviour on at least two occasions that causes another person alarm or distress. The person engaging in the behaviour knows, or ought to know, that their conduct amounts to harassment.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `harassment`

### 3. Sexual Offences (Sexual Offences Act 2003)

#### Rape
A rape occurs when a person uses their penis without consent to penetrate the vagina, mouth or anus of another person.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `rape`
- `sexual`

#### Sexual Assault
Sexual assault is where a person intentionally touches another sexually without their consent.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `sexual_assault`
- `sexual`

#### Assault by Penetration
Assault by penetration involves intentional penetration of the vagina or anus with any part of the body or an object without consent.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `assault_by_penetration`
- `sexual`

#### Child Sexual Abuse
Child sexual abuse occurs when a child (defined as under 18) is forced or incited to take part in sexual activity. The offences include:
- Physical acts (rape, oral sex, touching)
- Non-contact acts such as making the child watch sexual images
- Encouraging inappropriate behaviour
- Grooming via the internet

These offences can be committed by adults or other children.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `child_sexual_abuse`
- `sexual`

### 4. Child Abuse (Non-Sexual)

A child is anyone under 18. Abuse is defined as maltreatment that inflicts harm or fails to prevent harm; it may occur within a family, an institution or online. 

Abuse can involve:
- Violence
- Cruelty
- Neglect
- Abduction
- Causing/allowing death or serious physical harm

**Related crime types in system:**
- `child_abuse_non_sexual`
- `neglect`
- `physical`
- `psychological`

### 5. Honour-Based Abuse and Harmful Practices

#### Honour-Based Abuse (HBA)
HBA is not a specific offence but describes violence, threats, intimidation, coercion or abuse committed to protect or defend the honour of an individual, family or community. Such crimes often involve family or community members and aim to punish perceived breaches of cultural expectations.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `honor_based`

#### Forced Marriage
A forced marriage occurs when one or both people do not consent to the marriage and pressure or abuse is used to make the marriage happen. 

Pressure may include:
- Physical violence
- Threats
- Emotional or psychological abuse
- Financial control
- Making someone feel that refusal will bring shame

A forced marriage also occurs if one or both people cannot give valid consent because of:
- Age
- Learning disability
- Lack of mental capacity

The law defines forced-marriage offences as:
- Using violence, threats or coercion to compel someone into marriage
- Deceiving them to travel abroad to marry
- Making a person who lacks capacity marry
- Causing a person under 18 to marry

Forced marriage may be accompanied by other offences such as assault, kidnap, rape, blackmail and immigration-related crimes.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `forced_marriage`
- `honor_based`

#### Female Genital Mutilation (FGM)
FGM is a procedure where female genitals are injured or altered for no medical reason, causing severe pain and long-term harm. It can occur at various ages (infancy through to first pregnancy) and has no health benefits.

The World Health Organisation identifies four types:
- **Type 1:** Removal of part or all of the clitoris
- **Type 2:** Removal of part or all of the clitoris and inner/outer labia
- **Type 3:** Narrowing the vaginal opening by cutting and stitching the labia (infibulation)
- **Type 4:** Other harmful acts such as pricking, piercing or burning

**Related crime types in system:**
- `fgm`
- `honor_based`

### 6. Prostitution and Human Trafficking

#### Prostitution (Section 54(2), Sexual Offences Act 2003)
A "prostitute" (the legal term used) is a person who, on at least one occasion and whether or not compelled, offers or provides sexual services in return for payment.

The CPS guidance emphasises that the prosecution focus is on those who force, exploit or harm people involved in prostitution.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `prostitution_related`

#### Trafficking for Sexual Exploitation
A person commits an offence if they arrange or facilitate a victim's travel by recruiting, transporting, transferring, harbouring or receiving them with a view to the victim being sexually exploited.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `trafficking`
- `exploitation`

#### Controlling Prostitution for Gain
Occurs when someone intentionally controls activities relating to another person's prostitution with the expectation of gain. "Control" includes compulsion, coercion or force.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `prostitution_related`
- `exploitation`
- `coercive_control`

### 7. Obscene Publications

#### Obscene Publications Act 1959
Criminalises the publication of an obscene article. 

**Definitions:**
- **Article:** Any description of article containing or embodying matter to be read or looked at, any sound record, or any film or other record of pictures
- **Publication:** Distributed, sold, hired or transmitted electronically (publication can be to just one person)
- **Obscene:** An article is obscene if its effect taken as a whole tends to deprave and corrupt persons likely to read, see or hear it

Sending or transmitting extreme pornographic images or indecent publications may constitute an offence under the Act, particularly if the material is grossly offensive, indecent or menacing.

**Related crime types in system:**
- `obscene_publications`
- `digital`

## Behavioural Patterns in Online Messaging

Research on cyber-abuse and CPS examples (e.g., controlling/coercive behaviour, cyber-stalking and exploitation) suggest that perpetrators typically progress through stages, from seemingly benign contact to dangerous, violent threats. 

Behaviours may include:
- Grooming and manipulation
- Digital surveillance and monitoring
- Online harassment and threats
- Distribution of intimate images
- Use of technology to isolate and control victims
- Financial coercion via digital means
